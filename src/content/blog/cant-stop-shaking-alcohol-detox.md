The shaking starts in the hands first. A tremor that won’t settle, no matter how much you try to steady yourself. It gets worse when you haven’t had a drink in a few hours. Sometimes it spreads — to the arms, the jaw, the entire body. Sometimes it’s accompanied by sweating you can’t explain, a heart that’s beating too hard and too fast, a sense that something is deeply wrong.

If this is what you’re experiencing right now — or what someone you love is going through — this article is written for you.

Shaking after stopping or reducing alcohol is not just uncomfortable. It is one of the body’s most urgent warning signals. It means the nervous system is in distress, and in some cases, it means a medical emergency is unfolding. Understanding what’s happening — and what signs indicate that professional medical help is needed immediately — could be one of the most important things you read today.

## Why Alcohol Withdrawal Causes Shaking

To understand why the body shakes during alcohol withdrawal, it helps to understand what alcohol does to the brain over time.

Alcohol is a central nervous system depressant. When consumed regularly and heavily, the brain compensates by increasing its own excitatory activity — essentially ramping up neurological function to counteract alcohol’s suppressive effect. Over time, the brain recalibrates entirely to function with alcohol present. It needs it to maintain what now passes for normal.

When alcohol is suddenly removed or significantly reduced, that compensatory hyperactivity has nothing to suppress it. The nervous system goes into overdrive — producing a cascade of symptoms driven by neurological overexcitation. Tremors are among the first and most consistent signs of this process. [According to Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/diseases-and-conditions/alcohol-withdrawal-a-to-z), shaking typically begins within 5 to 10 hours after the last drink and peaks between 24 and 48 hours.

The shaking itself is not the primary danger. It’s what the shaking signals — and what may come next — that makes alcohol withdrawal a medical situation, not simply an uncomfortable one.

## 4 Dangerous Signs You Need Alcohol Detox Now

Tremors alone indicate that withdrawal is underway. But the following four signs, individually or in combination, indicate that the situation has moved beyond something to manage at home and into territory that requires immediate medical attention.

### Sign 1: Tremors Accompanied by Racing Heart and Spiking Blood Pressure

Shaking is rarely the only symptom of significant alcohol withdrawal. It typically arrives alongside a cluster of symptoms driven by the same neurological hyperactivity — and among the most dangerous of those are cardiovascular effects.

A racing pulse, elevated blood pressure, rapid breathing, and profuse sweating appearing alongside tremors indicate that the autonomic nervous system is severely dysregulated. These are not symptoms to wait out. Cardiovascular instability during alcohol withdrawal can escalate rapidly, and heart arrhythmias — irregular heart rhythms — are among the leading causes of death in severe, untreated withdrawal.

According to [clinical criteria published by the National Institutes of Health](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK441882/), a systolic blood pressure above 150 mmHg combined with a heart rate over 100 beats per minute are recognized indicators of severe withdrawal that require inpatient medical management. If you or someone nearby is experiencing shaking alongside a pounding or irregular heartbeat, chest tightness, or difficulty breathing, this is a medical emergency. Call 911 or get to an emergency room immediately.

In a medically supervised detox setting, these cardiovascular symptoms are closely monitored and managed with medications that stabilize the nervous system and protect the heart throughout the withdrawal process.

### Sign 2: Hallucinations — Seeing, Hearing, or Feeling Things That Aren’t There

One of the most alarming — and least expected — symptoms of alcohol withdrawal is hallucinations. Many people don’t realize this is a recognized withdrawal symptom, and when it happens, it can be terrifying for both the person experiencing it and anyone around them.

Alcohol withdrawal hallucinations typically begin within 12 to 24 hours after the last drink. They can be visual — commonly described as seeing insects, small animals, or moving objects that aren’t real. They can be auditory — hearing voices, sounds, or noises with no external source. They can also be tactile — a crawling sensation on the skin, or the feeling of being touched when no one is there.

It’s important to distinguish alcohol hallucinosis from delirium tremens (discussed below). A person experiencing alcohol hallucinosis may still be alert and aware that what they’re perceiving isn’t real — though this is deeply distressing. Delirium tremens involves a more complete loss of contact with reality.

Either way, hallucinations during alcohol withdrawal are a clear signal that the central nervous system is in serious distress and that medical intervention is urgently needed. This is not a symptom to manage at home. It is a sign that withdrawal has progressed to a stage where medical supervision — and the medications used to stabilize neurological function — are necessary.

### Sign 3: Seizures

Alcohol withdrawal seizures are one of the most dangerous complications that can occur when a person with significant physical dependence stops drinking — and they can happen with terrifying speed.

Seizures during alcohol withdrawal typically occur between 6 and 48 hours after the last drink. They are usually generalized tonic-clonic seizures — the type most people associate with the word “seizure” — involving loss of consciousness, rigid muscles, and rhythmic convulsive movements. They can occur as a single event or in clusters over a period of hours.

Approximately 3 to 5% of people undergoing alcohol withdrawal will experience seizures if the process is not medically managed. That percentage sounds small until you consider the scale: an estimated 2 million Americans experience alcohol withdrawal symptoms every year. And critically, seizures during withdrawal are not predictable from tremors alone — a person can appear to be managing relatively mild symptoms and then seize without warning.

A withdrawal seizure is a medical emergency, full stop. If a seizure occurs, call 911 immediately. Additionally, a history of previous withdrawal seizures is one of the strongest risk factors for experiencing another — and each episode of withdrawal can be more severe than the last, a phenomenon researchers call kindling.

This escalating pattern is one of the most important reasons why people who have been through alcohol withdrawal before — especially those who have experienced seizures — should never attempt to detox without medical supervision.

### Sign 4: Confusion, Disorientation, or Extreme Agitation — Signs of Delirium Tremens

Delirium tremens, commonly known as the DTs, is the most severe and potentially life-threatening stage of alcohol withdrawal. It represents the extreme end of the withdrawal spectrum, and it requires emergency medical care.

Delirium tremens typically emerges 48 to 96 hours after the last drink, though it can develop later. Its hallmarks are severe, coarse whole-body tremors, profound confusion and disorientation, hallucinations that the person cannot distinguish from reality, fever, extreme agitation, and dangerous cardiovascular instability. A person experiencing DTs may not know where they are, who they are with, or what is happening to them.

Without treatment, delirium tremens carries a mortality rate of up to 37%. With appropriate inpatient medical care, that rate drops to less than 5%. The difference between those two numbers is medical supervision, appropriate medications, and a clinical team that knows what to watch for and how to respond.

DTs do not announce themselves clearly in advance. They can develop from what appeared to be a manageable withdrawal. Risk factors that increase the likelihood of DTs include a history of prior DT episodes, heavy and prolonged daily drinking, older age, co-occurring medical conditions, and previous withdrawal seizures — but DTs can and do occur in people without obvious risk factors.

If someone shows signs of extreme confusion, severe uncontrollable shaking, fever, or appears to have lost contact with reality during alcohol withdrawal, call 911. Do not wait to see if symptoms improve.

## The Critical Window: Why Timing Matters

One of the most dangerous misconceptions about alcohol withdrawal is that the worst will pass if you just wait it out. For some people with mild dependence, that may be true. But for anyone with significant physical dependence — and the presence of the symptoms described in this article is a strong indicator of that — the withdrawal process can escalate unpredictably over the first 72 hours and beyond.

The window in which withdrawal becomes most dangerous is also the window in which medical intervention is most effective. Medications used in supervised detox — particularly benzodiazepines — work specifically by calming the neurological overactivity that drives tremors, seizures, and delirium tremens. When administered early and appropriately, they can prevent the most dangerous complications from developing at all.

Waiting — hoping the shaking will stop on its own, reasoning that you’ve gotten through withdrawal before — is a gamble with consequences that can be permanent or fatal. The responsible choice, when these signs are present, is medical care.
