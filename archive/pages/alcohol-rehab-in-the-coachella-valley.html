What Is Alcohol Rehab in the Coachella Valley?

Alcohol rehab is a structured treatment program designed specifically for those struggling with alcohol use disorder (AUD). Unlike simply stopping drinking, residential alcohol rehab addresses the physical, psychological, and emotional dimensions of addiction.
At New Beginnings, our residential alcohol program includes:

  • 24/7 medical and clinical support throughout treatment
  • A structured daily schedule that rebuilds routine and stability
  • Evidence-based therapies tailored to each individual
  • A safe, trigger-free environment away from the stressors of daily life
  • Whole-person wellness that nurtures body and mind

For many people, residential rehab is the most effective first step — providing the focus and support that outpatient care alone cannot offer.

Signs Alcohol Rehab May be Needed 

Alcohol use disorder can develop gradually, and it isn’t always easy to recognize. Common signs that professional rehab may be appropriate include:

  • Inability to control or stop drinking despite wanting to
  • Continuing to drink even as relationships, work, or health suffer
  • Needing to drink more to feel the same effects (tolerance)
  • Experiencing withdrawal symptoms — shaking, sweating, anxiety — when not drinking
  • Giving up activities that were once important in order to drink
    Drinking in secret or feeling shame around alcohol use

If any of these feel familiar, our team is here to help — without judgment.

Alcohol Withdrawal: Why Medical Support Matters

Alcohol withdrawal is one of the most medically serious phases of recovery. Unlike many other substances, stopping alcohol abruptly can trigger life-threatening complications — including seizures — if not properly managed.

Medical Alert

Alcohol withdrawal can cause seizures, severe dehydration, and a condition called delirium tremens (DTs) — a medical emergency. This is why attempting to quit alcohol without medical supervision can be dangerous.

At New Beginnings Recovery, our licensed clinical team monitors every client around the clock during this critical phase — ensuring safety and making the process as comfortable as possible. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between alcohol detox and alcohol rehab?

Detox is the first step — safely clearing alcohol from the body under medical supervision. Rehab begins after detox and focuses on the psychological, emotional, and behavioral aspects of recovery. Most clients benefit from completing both stages, and at New Beginnings, we offer a seamless transition between the two.

How do I know if I need residential alcohol rehab?

Residential rehab is typically recommended if you’ve tried to stop drinking on your own without success, if you experience withdrawal symptoms when you don’t drink, if alcohol is affecting your health, relationships, or work, or if you lack a stable and supportive home environment. Our admissions team can help assess the right level of care for your situation.

Is alcohol rehab covered by insurance?

Many major insurance plans cover residential alcohol rehab. Our admissions team can verify your benefits quickly and confidentially at no cost — so you can focus on getting help rather than navigating paperwork.

How long does alcohol rehab last?

Most clients start with a 30-day program, while others benefit from 60 or 90 days depending on their history, goals, and clinical needs. Longer stays are associated with stronger long-term recovery outcomes, and our team will recommend a personalized timeline during the admissions process.

What happens during a typical day in alcohol rehab?

Each day is structured around therapy, wellness, and community. Mornings typically include individual or group therapy sessions. Afternoons focus on skill-building, relapse prevention, or family sessions. Evenings include community meals, mindfulness practices, or quiet time — all designed to rebuild routine and a sense of purpose.
